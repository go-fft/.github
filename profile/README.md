# go-fft

🌐 **[Website](https://go-fft.github.io)** · 📚 **[Documentation](https://go-fft.github.io/docs/)** · ▶️ **[Playground](https://go-fft.github.io/playground/)**

**A pure-Go (no cgo) FFT library**, the `numpy.fft` / `scipy.fft` equivalent for
Go. Complex and real transforms of any length, N-D transforms, numpy's
`n`/`norm`/`axes`, the DCT and DST, single precision, windows and frequency
helpers, with no dependency on the FFTW3 C library.

It runs on the six 64-bit Go targets (amd64, arm64, riscv64, loong64, ppc64le,
s390x). The passes of its Stockham engine run as AVX2 and AVX-512 kernels on
amd64 and NEON kernels on arm64, generated by
[go-asmgen](https://github.com/go-asmgen) and bit-identical to the Go code they
replace. Since v0.28.0, `Options{FMA: true}` opts into fused multiply-add
kernels on amd64: as accurate, not bit-identical, and up to 1.22× faster.

on Intel CPUs with AVX-512 when an axis other than the last has a length 9·p
([v0.26.3 release notes](https://github.com/go-fft/fft/releases/tag/v0.26.3)).

## How it compares

Measured against FFTW, numpy and scipy on three GCC Compile Farm hosts (Zen 3,
Neoverse-N1, Cascade Lake), with every transform first checked against
`numpy.fft`. At v0.27.0, go-fft is faster than FFTW on all 24 rows on
Neoverse-N1, within 5% of it or faster on 18 on Zen 3 (faster on 17) and on 15
on Cascade Lake (faster on 14, every 2-D row among them), and at or above numpy.fft and scipy.fft on all 24 rows on every host. With the
opt-in FMA plans (v0.28.0), complex 1,024 on Cascade Lake runs at 0.94× FFTW's
time (1.19× by default). The tables, raw runs and the dated optimization rounds, including
what was tried and dropped, are in
[BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/go-fft/fft/blob/main/BENCHMARKS.md) and on the
[performance page](https://go-fft.github.io/docs/performance/).
